What this part of BouyUp does

BouyUp lends to e-commerce sellers through five AI agents. This service is the shared, tamper-proof record the agents, the partner banks and auditors rely on.

BouyUp is the third party in the middle: it pays the user at once from its own funds, then claims each bank's share back from that bank. The record on chain is the proof of payment named in every bank's contract.

A withdrawal, step by step

  1. Agent 4 asks for a payout.It sends the user, the account number, the amount, the region and province, the split between banks in percent, and who is asking. A staff member can do the same by hand, with a reason.
  2. The split is checked.Each percent has at most 2 decimals and the total is exactly 100.00%. Every bank must exist, be active, belong to the region and have an encryption key. Anything else is refused with a clear error.
  3. BouyUp pays the account.Today this is a mock treasury that always pays; a real bank transfer plugs in at the same spot.
  4. The record is cut into pieces and encrypted.One summary piece, plus one piece per bank with that bank's percent and amount. Each piece gets its own key.
  5. Keys go into unnamed slots.One slot per reader: BouyUp's master key, and each bank. A bank's slot holds the summary key and its own piece key, nothing more.
  6. The chain stores the sealed record.The encrypted envelope, a fingerprint of every piece and their combined fingerprint. The contract recomputes the combined fingerprint and refuses a mismatch.
  7. Four nodes keep a copy.The response carries the transaction hash, block, the node that sealed it and how many nodes already hold it.
  8. Each bank reads and proves its share.With its private key a bank opens its slot, reads its piece and checks it against the fingerprint on chain. Its share stays pending until BouyUp claims it from the bank.

The master key and the bank keys decrypt withdrawals. The chain signing keys write to the chain but cannot read anything encrypted. The reference salt turns account and staff references back into real ids. In this demo every key lives in the file set by bankKeysFile, created on first start. Limits and config

  
What is not hidden

  
    
    
  • That a withdrawal happened, when, its id, the number of pieces (banks + 1) and the record size.
    • 
    
  • If a key leaks, every record locked to it stays readable for good: a chain cannot be wiped. The master key is the most sensitive secret.
    • 
    
  • A bank sees only its own share, so it cannot check alone that the shares add up to 100%. The service checks before writing, and anyone with the master key can re-check with GET /v1/withdrawals/{id}/verify.
    • 
    
  • The payout is a mock treasury; real transfers and reimbursement claims are outside this service.
    •

When a bank changes its key

  
The bank sends its new public key to PUT /v1/banks/{code}/public-key. New withdrawals use it; older ones stay readable with the key that was current then. Keeping old keys is the bank's job.
